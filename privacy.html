Privacy policy

Last updated: 26 November 2026

Miaad (ميعاد) shows people who live in the UAE all their credit cards and bills in one place. It’s run by Miaad, Mohamed Bin Zayed City, MBZ 19, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates (“Miaad”, “we”). This policy explains what we collect, why, where it’s kept and the choices you have. It covers the Miaad apps and this website.

Miaad is in a private test with invited people, so some features described here may change. When they do, we’ll update this page.

What we collect

When you sign in

  • Your mobile number, to send you a sign-in code and to recognise you.
  • The name and type of each device you sign in on, so sessions can be seen and ended.
  • Invite codes you use or share.

Your cards and bills

  • For each card: the bank, the last four digits, a name you give it and its credit limit. Never the full card number: the app won’t accept one.
  • For two banks only, Miaad collects more of the card number: the last 6 digits of an HSBC India card, or the last 8 of a Standard Chartered India card. These banks’ statements open only with them. Miaad asks for them only when one of their statements needs them, to give you the smart statement of your card spending. We save them encrypted, use them only to open that card’s statements, never show or share them, and delete them when you remove the card.
  • If you scan a card, your phone reads it and Miaad keeps only the bank, the card’s name and the last four digits. The full number is checked and dropped on the phone, and the picture is never saved or sent. On iPhone, Apple’s Vision does the reading. On Android, Google’s ML Kit does it inside the app, and sends Google diagnostics such as the phone model and how long the reading took, never the picture or what it read.
  • If you scan a cheque, your phone reads it and Miaad keeps only the amount, the date, the cheque number, who it’s paid to and the bank, for you to check before saving. The account number printed on the cheque isn’t kept, and the picture is never saved or sent.
  • For each bill: the statement and due dates, the total and minimum due, and whether you’ve marked it paid.
  • Cards can be from banks in the UAE or, starting with India, from abroad. A card’s bills stay in its own currency.
  • For each utility account (electricity and water, phone and internet, Salik and district cooling): the provider, the account number, and for each bill its date, due date and amount due, and whether it’s paid. For Salik, the balance. We keep the full account number encrypted, so you’ll be able to pay the bill through Miaad, and only ever show its last four digits. Removing the account in the app deletes it.
  • Payments you add yourself, such as cheques, rent or school fees: what you enter (what it’s for, a name, who it’s to, the amount, the date, a cheque number and bank, a note) and the days you want reminding. They’re used only to show and remind you of them, and deleting one in the app deletes it. Don’t enter card or account numbers: the app won’t accept them.

If you connect your email (optional)

  • With your permission, Miaad gets read-only access to your Gmail or Outlook mailbox.
  • We only look for statement and payment-confirmation emails from our list of banks’ own email domains, and for bill, payment and balance emails from our list of utility providers’ own domains: DEWA, SEWA, TAQA Distribution, Etihad Water & Electricity, e&, du, Virgin Mobile, Salik, Empower and Tabreed. We only open those your email provider confirms really came from the bank or provider. We don’t read your other emails.
  • From those emails and their PDF statements we take the bill summary (the card’s last four digits, the statement and due dates, and the amounts due) and each transaction on the statement: its date, its description as printed, and its amount. We use the transactions only for your smart statement, which shows what you spent by category, the charges and EMIs on your card, and your payments and refunds. They’re deleted when you remove the card, and never used for ads or offers. From a payment confirmation we take the card’s last four digits, the amount paid and the date, to show the bill as paid. We don’t keep the emails or their attachments. To read each email only once, we keep a fingerprint of its ID, the bank and the result.
  • From a utility provider’s emails, and any bill attached without a password, we take the account number, the bill date, the due date and the amount due. From a payment confirmation, the amount paid and the date, to show the bill as paid. From Salik, the balance. Our own code reads them: they’re never sent to an AI model.
  • We keep the access token Google or Microsoft gives us, encrypted, and delete it when you disconnect.
  • Your email account’s profile picture, to show as your photo in Miaad. It comes with an Outlook connection. For Gmail, we take it only if you tap “Use my Google photo” on your profile: Google then asks you to share your name and profile picture, and we keep only the picture. We use it for nothing else, keep it in the UAE, and delete it when you disconnect.

If you turn on payment SMS (optional, Android only)

  • On an Android phone you can let Miaad read payment SMS. It’s off until you turn it on, and your phone asks your permission too.
  • Miaad’s code on your phone looks only at SMS from our list of banks’ and utility providers’ sender names, such as Emirates NBD, ADCB, FAB, DEWA, du and e&. It ignores every other SMS, including one-time codes and purchase alerts.
  • From a payment or due-date SMS it takes the bank or provider, the last four digits of the card or account, the amount, the date, and whether it was a payment or a due date. Only those details are sent to Miaad, stored in the UAE, to show your bills as paid. The text of your SMS never leaves your phone, and we never store it or send it to an AI model.
  • Turn it off at any time in the app, or take away the permission in your phone’s settings.

Your details for opening statements

Banks lock statement PDFs with a password made from personal details, such as the first letters of your name and your date of birth. If you give us your name (in English letters) and date of birth, we use them to open your statements automatically. We work out each password when a statement arrives and never store it.

Consents

A record of each consent you give or withdraw, for example to the terms or to email access, with the date and the version of the wording.

If you join the waitlist on this website

  • Your name, email address and UAE mobile number, and, if you tell us, which other countries you have credit cards from.
  • We use them to invite you to Miaad, and the countries to decide which to add next.
  • They’re kept in the UAE, and deleted 12 months after you send them, or sooner if you ask.
  • When a request arrives, our team gets an email that only says a request arrived. It doesn’t include your details.

This website

No accounts, cookies, analytics or advertising trackers. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, which keeps standard server logs, such as IP addresses, for security. The waitlist form sends what you type straight to Miaad’s servers in the UAE.

How we use it

  • To run Miaad: show your cards, your bills and what’s due, remind you of due dates, and help you avoid interest and late fees.
  • Reminders come in the app, by WhatsApp unless you turn that off, and as notifications on your phone if you allow them. Phone notifications are scheduled on the phone itself: nothing about them is sent anywhere.
  • To sign you in and keep your account secure.
  • To answer you when you contact us.

We don’t sell your data, and we don’t use it for advertising.

Google user data. Miaad’s use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. We use Gmail data only to show you your own cards and bills, and your Google profile picture only as your photo in the app. Neither is ever used for ads or offers. People at Miaad don’t read it, except when you ask us to (for example, to fix a problem), for security, or when the law requires it.

Reading statements with AI

Our own code reads most statements. When it can’t, we send the statement’s text to Anthropic’s Claude model to read the bill summary and its transactions, to prepare your smart statement, which shows what you spent by category, the charges and EMIs on your card. Before anything is sent, we remove your name, card numbers, account numbers, Emirates ID, phone numbers and email addresses, and on statements from India, PAN and Aadhaar numbers. Anthropic processes this masked text in the United States under its commercial terms, which don’t allow it to use our data to train its models.

Where it’s kept

Your data is stored in the UAE, on Microsoft Azure’s UAE North region in Dubai. The exception is the masked statement text that Anthropic reads in the United States.

Our WhatsApp number uses WhatsApp’s UAE local storage, so the content of our WhatsApp messages is stored only in the UAE, although Meta may process it outside the UAE for up to 60 minutes while delivering it.

How long we keep it

During the private test we keep your data until the test ends, then delete it, unless you choose to stay on as a member. You can ask us to delete it sooner at any time.

Your choices and rights

  • Disconnect your email at any time in the app. For Gmail, disconnecting also withdraws Miaad’s access in your Google account.
  • Remove any card or bill account in the app.
  • Ask us for a copy of your data, to correct it, or to delete it and your account: write to hello@miaad.club. We reply within 30 days.
  • Under the UAE’s Personal Data Protection Law you can also object to how we use your data and complain to the UAE Data Office.

Security

  • Data is encrypted in transit and at rest.
  • Email access tokens and utility account numbers are encrypted again with our own key.
  • We never store full card numbers or statement passwords.
  • Only masked text ever reaches an AI model.

Children

Miaad is for adults, 18 and over, who live in the UAE.

Changes

When this policy changes we’ll update this page and its date, and tell you in the app about important changes.
